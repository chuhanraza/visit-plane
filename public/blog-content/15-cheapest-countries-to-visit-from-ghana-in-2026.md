## Overview

Ghanaian passport holders sit in one of Africa's most travel-friendly positions. Accra is a launchpad for the ECOWAS region, where free-movement arrangements and a dense web of shared taxis, intercity coaches, and short regional flights put a dozen countries within easy reach. The cedi stretches a long way across West Africa, especially once you are eating where locals eat and sleeping in family-run guesthouses rather than international chains. The result is that some of the cheapest, richest trips you can take barely require a flight at all.

## How These Trips Stay Cheap

The single biggest saving for Ghanaian travellers is staying regional. ECOWAS overland travel — shared taxis and STC or private intercity coaches from Accra to Lomé, Cotonou, and beyond — removes the flight cost that usually dominates a budget. For destinations a little further out, regional flights between West African hubs are often cheaper than long-haul connections.

Timing matters too. Travelling in shoulder season, outside major festivals and school holidays, brings down both airfares and accommodation. On the ground, the old rules hold: eat where locals eat, choose family-run guesthouses over international chains, and use shared or public transport. Finally, carry CFA francs for the francophone neighbours — Togo, Benin, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, and Burkina Faso all use the currency, and having cash on hand avoids poor exchange rates and card-acceptance headaches.
