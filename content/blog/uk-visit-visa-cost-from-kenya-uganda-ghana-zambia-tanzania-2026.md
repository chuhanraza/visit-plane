## Overview

The headline price of a UK visit visa is simple. The real cost, in shillings, cedis or kwacha, is not, because it depends on exchange rates, bank charges and a handful of extras people forget to budget for. This guide breaks down what Kenyan, Ugandan, Ghanaian, Zambian and Tanzanian applicants actually pay for a UK **Standard Visitor visa** in 2026, where the hidden costs are, and how to avoid paying more than you need to.

If you want the full application walkthrough rather than the cost breakdown, see our country guides: [Kenya](/blog/kenya-to-united-kingdom-visa-requirements-how-to-apply-2026), [Uganda](/blog/uganda-to-united-kingdom-visa-requirements-how-to-apply-2026), [Ghana](/blog/ghana-to-united-kingdom-visa-requirements-how-to-apply-2026), [Zambia](/blog/zambia-to-united-kingdom-visa-requirements-how-to-apply-2026) and [Tanzania](/blog/tanzania-to-united-kingdom-visa-requirements-how-to-apply-2026).

> **Key takeaway:** The UK Standard Visitor visa for up to six months costs **£135**, set by the UK government and charged in pounds. Your cost in local currency is that fee multiplied by the day's exchange rate, plus your bank or card charges, plus smaller extras such as photos, printing, translations and travel to the biometrics centre. The fee is non-refundable if you are refused, so a complete, well-prepared application is the best way to protect your money.

## The Official Fee: £135

For a stay of up to six months, the fee is **£135** per applicant. It applies to every adult and child, so a family of four pays four fees. Longer visit visas, valid for two, five or ten years, cost considerably more, and each visit is still limited to six months. Do not choose a long-term visa unless you genuinely expect to travel to the UK regularly: you pay far more up front, and the refusal risk is the same.

Fees are set by the UK government and change from time to time, usually once a year. Always confirm the current amount on GOV.UK before you pay, and treat any figure on a third-party website, ours included, as a guide.

## Turning £135 Into Local Currency

You pay in pounds, and your bank or card converts it at that day's rate. The simple way to estimate:

1. Check the **current pound exchange rate** for your currency on your bank's app or a rate site.
2. Multiply by 135.
3. Add **2% to 4%** for card or conversion charges as a cautious allowance, and more if you pay with a card that adds foreign-transaction fees.

Exchange rates can move noticeably in a few weeks, so check the rate on the day you pay, and make sure the card you use is enabled for international online payments before you start. A declined payment partway through the form is a common, avoidable delay.

## What the Fee Covers, and What It Does Not

The visa fee covers the decision on your application, and standard biometric enrolment at the visa application centre is part of the process. The things below are **not** included, and they add up:

- **Passport photos** to the required specification, if the centre or your photographer charges for them.
- **Printing and photocopies** of bank statements, letters and invitation documents.
- **Certified translations** of any document not in English.
- **Travel and accommodation** for the biometrics appointment if you do not live in the centre city.
- **Courier or passport-return services**, where offered, which are optional.
- **Optional service-centre extras** such as priority appointments or additional services. These are optional; you do not need them to apply.
- **Travel insurance** for the trip. Visitors can be charged for NHS treatment, so cover is sensible even though it is not part of the visa application.

## Priority Services: Are They Worth It?

Faster decision services may be available for an extra fee, and availability varies by country and time of year. They speed up the **decision**, not your odds of approval. A faster decision on a weak file is still a refusal. If you have a real deadline, apply earlier instead of paying to compress the wait, and only buy a priority service when your documents are already complete and you can afford the extra cost without weakening the rest of your budget.

## Do You Need an Agent?

You do not need an agent to apply, and the official fee is the same whether you apply yourself or not. Agents charge their own fees on top, and some charge a lot. If you use one, make sure you know exactly what they are paid for, that you pay the visa fee yourself on the official GOV.UK payment page, and that you personally check every detail on the form, because you are the one who signs the declaration. Be careful with anyone who promises approval or guarantees a result. No one can.

## The Cost of a Refusal

The visa fee is **not refunded** if your application is refused, and a new application means paying again. That makes preparation the cheapest saving available. The most common avoidable causes of refusal are unexplained deposits, weak ties to your home country, inconsistent details and missing documents, all of which are covered in our country guides. Spending an extra week assembling a clean file costs far less than a second fee.

## Country Notes

**Kenya.** Biometrics are taken in Nairobi. If you live in Mombasa, Kisumu or Eldoret, factor in transport and a night's accommodation. M-Pesa statements can support your financial picture but do not replace bank statements.

**Uganda.** Biometrics are taken in Kampala. Applicants from Gulu, Mbarara or Jinja should budget for the journey. Mobile money statements support your file but should match your declared income.

**Ghana.** Biometrics are taken in Accra. Applicants from Kumasi, Tamale or Takoradi should plan travel around the appointment. Keep GRA and SSNIT documents tidy and consistent.

**Zambia.** Biometrics are taken in Lusaka. Applicants from Ndola, Kitwe or Livingstone should budget for travel and plan the trip around the appointment slot.

**Tanzania.** Biometrics are taken in Dar es Salaam. Applicants from Arusha, Mwanza, Dodoma or Zanzibar should arrange transport and accommodation in advance.

## Paying Safely and Keeping Proof

Pay only on the official GOV.UK payment page, and check the web address before you enter card details: look-alike sites that charge extra "service fees" are one of the most common ways applicants lose money. Keep the payment receipt and the application reference number together in one folder, on your phone and in print. If a payment fails, check with your bank before retrying so you are not charged twice, and never share your card PIN or one-time passwords with anyone claiming to help with your application. If you are paying from a business or relative's card, make sure the cardholder knows what the charge is for, and keep their consent in writing in case a bank query arises.

## Common Ways People Overpay

- **Paying on an unofficial website** that adds a "service" charge for a form you could complete for free on GOV.UK.
- **Paying an agent** for something you can do yourself.
- **Choosing a long-term visa** you will not use.
- **Using a card with high foreign charges** when a better option exists.
- **Applying incompletely**, getting refused and paying twice.
- **Buying non-refundable flights** before the visa arrives.
