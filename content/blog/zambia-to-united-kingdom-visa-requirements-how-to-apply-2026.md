## Overview

Zambian passport holders need a visa before travelling to the United Kingdom. For a holiday, a visit to family or friends, or a short business trip, that means the **Standard Visitor visa**. This guide explains the 2026 rules in plain language: the fee, what to submit, how biometrics work in Lusaka, how long a decision takes, and what separates approvals from refusals for Zambians.

> **Key takeaway:** Zambians need a Standard Visitor visa for the UK. The fee is **£135** for a stay of up to six months. There is no fixed bank balance to hit: the Home Office wants to see that you are a genuine visitor, that you can pay for the trip, and that your life in Zambia gives you every reason to come home. Apply online, give biometrics in Lusaka, and do not book non-refundable travel until the visa is in your passport.

## Do Zambians Need a Visa for the UK?

Yes. Zambian citizens cannot enter the UK as visitors without a visa, and a visa is not something you can get on arrival. You must apply and receive a decision **before** you travel. The Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) system applies to visa-free nationalities only, so it is not the route for Zambians.

The Standard Visitor visa covers tourism, visiting family and friends, short business activities such as meetings and conferences, and some short courses of study. It does **not** allow you to work, study long term, or live in the UK. If your plans are bigger than a visit, you need a different visa and a different application.

## The Fee in 2026

The Standard Visitor visa for up to six months costs **£135**. Longer visit visas (two, five and ten years) cost more, and paid priority services exist for faster decisions. Fees are set by the UK government, change from time to time, and are charged in pounds, so what you actually pay in Zambian kwacha depends on the exchange rate and your bank's charges that day. Always confirm the amount on GOV.UK before paying and keep the receipt.

Budget for more than the visa fee itself. Add the cost of photocopies and printing, certified translations of any document not in English, a possible courier or travel to Lusaka for biometrics, and travel insurance for the trip. These are small next to the fee, but forgetting them is how applicants end up rushing.

## Step by Step: How to Apply

1. **Check you are applying for the right visa.** Visit, not work or study.
2. **Complete the online application** on GOV.UK and pay the fee. Apply no more than three months before you intend to travel.
3. **Book your biometrics appointment** at the visa application centre in Lusaka and bring the appointment confirmation and whatever the appointment email asks for.
4. **Upload or present your documents** as instructed, in order and clearly labelled.
5. **Wait for the decision.** Standard decisions usually arrive within about three weeks of biometrics, though busy seasons can run longer.
6. **Check the visa details** (name, dates and permitted activities) when the decision arrives, and only then pay for non-refundable flights and accommodation.

Biometrics are taken in Lusaka, so applicants from Ndola, Kitwe, Livingstone or Kabwe should arrange travel around the appointment rather than leaving it until the last minute.

## Documents You Will Need

The Home Office publishes the list, and caseworkers look for a coherent story rather than a pile of paper. As a baseline:

- A **valid passport** with blank pages, plus previous passports if they show earlier travel.
- A **recent bank statement** history (typically the last three to six months), with a name and account number that match your application.
- **Proof of your job or business:** an employment letter stating your role, salary and leave dates, plus payslips, or a PACRA business registration certificate and business bank statements.
- **Tax and social security records** such as a ZRA TPIN and tax records and a NAPSA contribution statement, which show a settled working life.
- **Evidence of accommodation and plans:** a hotel booking or an invitation from a relative or friend in the UK with their address and status.
- **Anything that shows you will return:** see the ties section below.

Documents not in English need a certified translation. Photocopies are fine unless the instructions say otherwise, but carry originals to the appointment in case they are requested.

## Proof of Funds: What the Home Office Actually Looks For

There is **no official minimum balance** for a UK visitor visa, and anyone who quotes you a magic number is guessing. What matters is whether your money matches your trip: enough to cover flights, accommodation and daily costs for the length of stay you are asking for, and enough left to keep your commitments at home.

- Show a **history**, not a last-minute balance. Three to six months of regular activity reads as real; a single large deposit a week before applying reads as borrowed.
- **Explain unusual deposits** with a short note and a document, such as a sale agreement, a bonus letter or a transfer record.
- If someone else is paying, include their **written support**, proof of their status in the UK and their own bank statements. Their funds are assessed too.

Mobile money (MTN and Airtel Money) is common in Zambia and a statement can support the picture of your income and spending. It works best as **supporting evidence next to bank statements**, not as a replacement. Keep the figures consistent with your declared income and explain any large or irregular deposits with proof of where they came from.

## Ties to Zambia: The Part That Decides Most Cases

The key question behind every refusal is simple: will this person leave the UK at the end of the visit? You answer it with ties that are real and verifiable.

Strong Zambian ties are specific and checkable: an employment letter and payslips, a ZRA TPIN and tax record, NAPSA contributions, a business registered with PACRA, a title deed or lease, savings with a recognised institution, dependants at school, or studies you will resume. Choose what is genuinely true for you and document it well.

A short cover letter helps pull everything together. Say who you are, why you are going, where you will stay, how long for, who is paying, and what you are returning to in Zambia, including the date you are due back at work or school. Keep it honest and consistent with the form, and keep it to a page.

## Visiting Family or Friends

If you are staying with a relative or friend in the UK, ask them for an **invitation letter** that states your relationship, the dates, the address, and that they know the rules for hosting a visitor. Their proof of status (passport page, residence document or similar) and proof of address, such as a council tax bill or tenancy agreement, make the letter credible. An invitation does not replace your own ties and funds: it supports them. Be clear about who pays for what.

## Tourists and Business Visitors

**Tourists** should show a realistic itinerary: where you will go, how long you will stay, and accommodation that fits your budget. You do not need fully paid-for bookings, and in fact refundable options are wiser until you have the visa. **Business visitors** should add a letter from your employer in Zambia explaining the purpose, the UK organisation you are meeting, who covers costs, and confirming you remain employed and will return. Remember that a visit visa does not permit paid work in the UK.

## Biometrics in Lusaka and Processing Times

After you apply online, you give fingerprints and a photograph at the visa application centre in Lusaka. Standard decisions typically come within about three weeks, but allow longer around the summer and December travel peaks, and when your file needs extra checks. Priority and super-priority services may be offered for an extra fee, and they speed up the decision, not the odds of approval. Plan around the standard timeline and treat anything faster as a bonus.

## How Long Can You Stay?

The Standard Visitor visa allows stays of **up to six months** per visit. Officers can grant less than you ask for, and the longer 2, 5 and 10-year visas still limit each stay to six months. A visa is permission to travel, not a guarantee of entry: border officers can ask about your plans, so keep your documents handy and answer plainly. Never overstay. It can damage future applications to the UK and other countries.

## Common Reasons Zambians Are Refused

- **Weak or unexplained ties** to Zambia: nothing obviously pulling you back.
- **Funds that do not add up:** sudden deposits, balances that do not fit the trip, or statements that contradict your declared income.
- **Inconsistent information:** dates, jobs or travel history that differ between the form, letters and statements.
- **An unclear purpose**, or a trip that looks like a way to stay.
- **Missing or unverifiable documents**, including untranslated papers.
- **Poor travel history without compensating strengths:** first-time applicants need especially clear ties and a modest, believable plan.

## If You Are Refused

A visit-visa refusal letter gives reasons, and the right to a formal appeal does not generally apply to visit visas. Read the reasons carefully, fix the actual problem (usually funds or ties), and reapply with a stronger file. Do not simply resubmit the same documents. Declare the earlier refusal honestly on every future application, because failing to do so creates a far bigger problem than the refusal itself.

- Invitation and host documents if staying with a relative or friend in the UK.
- Itinerary and refundable bookings.
- Cover letter, one page, consistent with the form.
- Certified translations for any non-English documents.
- Biometrics appointment in Lusaka booked.

For Zambians, the UK Standard Visitor visa is a document-and-credibility exercise. The £135 fee is the easy part. Prepare early, apply online, give biometrics in Lusaka, and keep your plans flexible until the visa arrives.
